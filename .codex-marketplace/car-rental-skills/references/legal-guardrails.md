## What the skills do and do not do

The agreement, inspection and business-plan skills produce working drafts and
checklists that an operator takes to their lawyer and insurer. They are not
legal advice, not insurance advice and not tax advice. Every deliverable from
those skills says so once, plainly, at the top, without burying the useful part
under disclaimers.

## Always flag

Mark these with "Confirm with your lawyer/insurer" in the draft:

- Liability caps, indemnity and hold-harmless language
- Damage, loss-of-use and diminished-value charges
- Security deposit holds and when they are released
- Insurance requirements: renter's own policy, credit card coverage (most cards
  exclude exotic and high-value cars), supplemental liability, physical damage
- Minimum age and young-driver rules
- Arbitration, venue and governing-law clauses
- Toll, ticket and camera-violation handling and admin fees
- Tracking devices and data collection (GPS, telematics, dash cams)
- Electronic signatures and ID checks

## Never write

- A citation to a statute, regulation or case the user did not supply. If a
  clause depends on state law, say "depends on <state> law" and stop there.
- A promise that a clause is enforceable.
- A clause that waives the operator's own negligence for injury, or that hides a
  fee in fine print (it is unenforceable in many states and it is the fastest
  way to a chargeback dispute).
- Discriminatory renter criteria (race, religion, national origin, sex,
  disability and other protected classes). Age and driving-record rules are
  normal; say they must be applied the same way to everyone.

## State-by-state

Rental rules differ by state (deposit handling, damage waivers, young-driver
rules, sales and rental taxes, peer-to-peer and commercial insurance mandates).
The skills ask which state the operator is in, list the topics that are usually
state-specific, and leave the answer to the operator's lawyer.
