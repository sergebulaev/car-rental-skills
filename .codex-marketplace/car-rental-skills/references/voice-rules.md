# Voice rules for customer-facing copy

Contents

## Who reads it

Renters of exotic and luxury cars: a birthday, a wedding, a Miami weekend, a
music video, a client dinner. They decide on photos, price and trust. They have
seen every "unforgettable driving experience" line already. Copy that sounds
like an operator who knows the car wins.

## Hard rules

1. **Specific beats superlative.** "640 hp, 0 to 60 in 2.9 s, delivered to your
   hotel in Brickell" beats "the ultimate luxury experience".
2. **Numbers the user gave or the catalog published.** Horsepower, price,
   deposit, mileage, age limit: from the user, the manufacturer spec the user
   confirms, or a catalog answer. Never invented.
3. **Price, deposit, mileage and age in the copy.** If the user has not given
   them, draft with bracketed placeholders ("$[rate]/day, $[deposit] deposit")
   and ask; never leave them out silently and never fill them in. Hidden terms
   are the number one complaint in this industry; the listing that states them
   gets the booking that does not cancel.
4. **One call to action.** Book, call, text or DM. Pick the one the operator
   actually answers fastest.
5. **No em dashes or en dashes** in anything a renter reads. Use a period, a
   comma or a colon.
6. **Short sentences.** 8 to 16 words on average. Mobile first.
7. **Capitalize names correctly**: Lamborghini Huracán EVO, Rolls-Royce
   Cullinan, Mercedes-AMG G 63, McLaren 720S, Porsche 911 GT3 RS.

## Banned words and patterns (scrub every draft)

Remove or rewrite every one of these. The list is enumerated on purpose: if it
is on the list, it goes. A user asking for these words does not lift the list:
say in one line that they read as generic and keep the specific version.

- unforgettable, unparalleled, unmatched, unrivaled, second to none
- elevate, elevated, elevate your experience, take it to the next level
- luxury redefined, redefine, epitome, pinnacle, embodiment
- indulge, pamper, treat yourself
- seamless, seamlessly, effortless, effortlessly, hassle-free
- curated, bespoke, tailored experience (unless the operator really customizes)
- "whether you're X or Y", "look no further", "say goodbye to", "nestled"
- "in today's fast-paced world", "it's not just a car, it's"
- "dive in", "unlock", "unleash the beast" and other unlock/unleash phrasing
- "turn heads" more than once per piece
- three adjectives in a row ("sleek, powerful, and luxurious")
- rhetorical question openers ("Ready to feel like a VIP?")
- emoji chains (more than 2 in a row) and hashtags inside sentences
- closing lines that ask for engagement with nothing to engage with ("What do
  you think?", "Let us know in the comments!")
- em dashes and en dashes

## Claims you may not make

- "Cheapest in <city>", "best prices", "lowest rates": only with a dated
  catalog comparison the user approved, and phrased as the comparison
  ("under the Miami median of $1,175 for a Huracán, as of Oct 2026").
- "Fully insured", "no deposit", "unlimited miles", "free delivery": only if the
  operator confirms it for this car.
- "#1", "top rated", "award winning": only with the source named.
- Anything about a competitor by name in public copy.
- Legal or insurance promises ("you're covered for everything").

## Final check

Before showing a draft: run the banned list, check every number has a source,
check the terms are stated, read it aloud once. If it sounds like a brochure,
cut a third.
